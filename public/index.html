The proposal

Why the school needs one

We ask for your approval to buy Saint Johannes Berchmans' first 3D printer, as one shared tool for every student and teacher.

  1. Approval in principle so the proposal can go to the principals and the Head of Academics.
  2. A budget line for the printer plus a small running budget for filament and spare parts.
  3. A suitable spot: a ventilated room or corner, a sturdy table and a power socket close by (possibly the same room as computer science / coding classes).

What a 3D printer does

A 3D printer builds a real object from a computer design, one thin layer of plastic at a time. A student draws it on screen, presses print, and holds it in their hand a few hours later. It is a school tool, not a club's equipment: anyone with an STJBS email can book it.

Why this matters

  • Students on teams. Greenmech and competition teams print, test, change and reprint within days.
  • Students in classes. STEAM, graphic design and computer science turn screen work into physical objects.
  • Students on passion projects. Build, test and photograph a finished project for a university portfolio.
  • Teachers. Printed solids, cross-sections and force models.
  • The school. More projects to show parents and applicants, more competition entries under the STJBS name.

STJBS has no 3D printing experience yet. That is why this proposal spends as much time on how the printer is controlled as on why we want it.

How it would run

Every print is tied to a named person from day one. I will build a simple booking and logging tool, open only to people who sign in with an STJBS email. It will run without any costs and be easy to maintain by the next generation of students.

  1. Sign in with your STJBS email and submit a request (file, material, how long, what for).
  2. First-time users complete a short safety induction.
  3. A teacher approves any unattended or overnight run.
  4. The tool books the slot and shows when the printer is free.

Sharing rules: competition deadlines get priority, classes get fixed slots, and passion projects fill the rest. Because every print is logged, the school gets a weekly or monthly report of who used the printer, for what, and for how long.

What STJBS gains

For students

  • A way to finish things
  • A portfolio piece
  • Practice in spatial thinking

For teachers

  • Printed models instead of a drawing on the board
  • A working link between code, design and the physical world

For the school

  • A stronger Greenmech program
  • More competition entries and results under the STJBS name
  • A visible culture of building, where success can be measured by more than tests and exams
  • A usage log that shows exactly how the investment is used

Next steps

  1. Your feedback, sir.
  2. Teacher support in writing.
  3. Student interest survey (STJBS emails only, one response each).
  4. Principals and the Head of Academics.
  5. Before any purchase, prices, bundle and stock are reconfirmed by official invoice and the room is checked for ventilation, power and table strength.

How I researched

How I chose

The question

Which printer fits which school job, and why.

Criteria, in order

  1. 1

    Filament capability

    Competition and engineering parts need carbon-fibre and nylon blends, so a printer that cannot run them cannot do the main job.

  2. 2

    Durability

    A shared school machine runs for years with many users; a fragile one costs more in repairs than it saves.

  3. 3

    Speed

    Teams print, test and reprint within days, so print time decides how many iterations fit before a deadline.

  4. 4

    Build size

    Bigger parts in one piece are stronger and simpler, but most school prints fit a mid-size bed.

  5. 5

    Price

    Last, because a cheap printer that fails the first four costs more over its life. Prices are compared per printer, never ranked.

Imports are kept in their own tier because shipping, import tax and warranty are unquoted.

What the research does not show

Proof that spatial training raises STEM grades is still preliminary (Stieff and Uttal, 2015). Student interest in makerspaces can also fade over a semester when support is low (Int. J. of STEM Education, 2018). We are not claiming a printer raises grades. We are claiming it is a strong way to practise a skill that predicts success, and the next section is how we keep interest up.

Which printer fits which job

Big parts

  • Anycubic Kobra S1 Max 42.9 L build volume.
  • QIDI Max4 51.7 L build volume.
  • QIDI Plus5 30.7 L build volume.

Rule: Build volume of 30 litres or more.

Classroom safety and air

  • Flashforge Creator 5 Pro HEPA13 + activated carbon.
  • Bambu Lab X2D 3-stage with H12 HEPA.
  • QIDI Max4 G3 + H12 HEPA + carbon.
  • QIDI Plus5 G3 + H12 HEPA + carbon.

Rule: A HEPA filter is fitted.

Multi-colour or multi-material

  • Anycubic Kobra S1 Max Optional colour system (ACE 2 Pro) sold separately, not in the quoted price.
  • Creality K2 Pro Optional colour system (CFS) sold separately, not in the quoted price.
  • Flashforge Creator 5 Pro 4 independent toolheads.

Rule: A stated colour system (even if sold separately) or 4 toolheads.

The recommendation

Main target: Anycubic Kobra S1 Max ★ Recommended

It is large, fully enclosed and heated, built for carbon-fibre materials, and sold here without a colour system, so the school pays for no extras.

  • About 35 cm print space, so a whole chassis part prints in one piece.
  • Heated, enclosed inside; nozzle built for tough materials.
  • Security: 3D Zaiku is a well-known 3D printer distributor in Indonesia and offers a one-year warranty on spare parts for this printer; terms and conditions must be reviewed.

Trade-off: about 2,200 W draw, so the room's power circuit must be checked first; the page says limited stock.

Backups, in order: Creality K2 Pro (first backup), Flashforge Creator 5 Pro, Bambu Lab P2S. Premium backup: QIDI Max4 (direct from QIDI).

For the principal and parents

Budget and safety

Power first. The Anycubic Kobra S1 Max draws about 2,200 W. The room's power circuit must be checked before buying.

Running costs

  • Spare nozzles, a filament dryer and filament are the running costs.
  • Hardened nozzles last roughly 200 to 500 hours on carbon fibre.
  • Local seller warranties cover spare parts for one year.
  • Total budget: to be confirmed by official invoice.

Ventilation and filtration

Printers with a HEPA filter fitted: Flashforge Creator 5 Pro (HEPA13 + activated carbon), Bambu Lab X2D (3-stage with H12 HEPA), QIDI Max4 (G3 + H12 HEPA + carbon), QIDI Plus5 (G3 + H12 HEPA + carbon). Whichever printer is bought, it goes in a ventilated room or corner.

Supervision

A named teacher approves any unattended or overnight run. First-time users complete a short safety induction.

Access and tracking

Only people who sign in with an STJBS email can book the printer, and every print is logged to a named person.

Before buying

  1. Set a filament budget and choose a vendor.
  2. Check the room's circuit (Kobra S1 Max about 2,200 W).
  3. For any QIDI import, get a delivered quote including shipping and import tax.

Imports

These ship from China. Shipping, import tax, voltage (choose the 220–240 V model) and warranty claims back to China are all unquoted. All three ship with a hardened-steel bimetal nozzle and hardened-steel extruder gears, so carbon fibre needs no extra parts. QIDI is one of the best for our school use case, but it must be imported. They offer good import services and consultation, so it is an option that should be considered.

Also considered as a premium backup: Prusa Core One (Rp 29,500,000 at 3D Zaiku; the listing title says "CORE ONE PLUS" while the code says "CORE ONE"; model, nozzle and reseller status unconfirmed).
